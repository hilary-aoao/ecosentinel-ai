How it works

Three steps, and a person always has the last word.

1

Detect

Two Sentinel-2 satellite images of the same ground are compared. Where dense vegetation turns into bare ground, the spot is flagged. Clouds and their shadows are masked out first.

2

Assess

Flags that sit close together become hotspots. Hotspots are ranked by size, so teams can start with the largest changes instead of searching the whole map.

3

Verify

Nothing is acted on automatically. Each hotspot is checked by a person, and the result is recorded: field visit, confirmed illegal, licensed or other, or false alarm.

Honest about the limits

This is a working prototype. Here is what it does, and what it cannot do yet.

What it does today

  • Compares two satellite images of the same 281 km² area.
  • Flags dense vegetation that became bare ground, and hides cloud and shadow.
  • Groups flags into hotspots and ranks them by size.
  • Keeps track of the human check for every hotspot.

What it cannot do yet

  • Tell legal from illegal. That needs the boundaries of licensed mines.
  • See through clouds. of the area was not checked in 2023.
  • Say who cleared the land or when. It has two dates only.
  • State its accuracy. It has not yet been tested against field-verified sites.
